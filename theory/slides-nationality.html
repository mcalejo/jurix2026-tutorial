One example carries the whole talk: two sections of the British Nationality Act 1981.

Sergot, Sadri, Kowalski, Kriwaczek, Hammond and Cory, The British Nationality Act as a Logic Program, Communications of the ACM, May 1986. The text is the Act as enacted. Later amendments changed the wording. The sentence already has the form of a rule: a conclusion, the word "if", and conditions. Parliament wrote a logic program without knowing it.

This is the one distinction of the first part. Every rule language makes it, or hides it. Both sentences say "shall". The first "shall" defines who is a citizen. The second "shall" gives the Secretary of State something to do.

A simplified rule, to show the form. The full rule comes in part 2. Backwards: is John a British citizen? He is, if he was born in the UK and a parent of his is a British citizen. Two smaller questions. Forwards: John is born in the UK, and his mother is a citizen. So John is a citizen. Prolog reasons backwards. Datalog reasons forwards. The rule is the same.

A belief can be false. A goal cannot be false: it can only be violated. Production rules, event-condition-action rules and the obligations of deontic logic are all goal rules. The constraint is section 12(3): a declaration "shall not be registered unless" the person will have another nationality. Nobody may be made stateless.

A person resolves these ambiguities without noticing. "His" is the person who is born, not the father of the father. "is a British citizen": the parent must be a citizen at the time of the birth, not today. Logical English gives up elegant variation. It keeps the meaning. It can be read without training in mathematics, in computing or in logic.

In part 2, time was a place of the templates: "is a British citizen on a date". Here the time is not written. A fluent holds from the event that starts it to the event that ends it. LPS: Logic Production Systems, with Fariba Sadri. It combines logic programs with production rules, and gives them one logical meaning. In the program each template is on one line; the lines are broken here to fit the slide.

Section 1(1) again, with no dates. "At the time of the birth" is the moment of the event. A fluent that no event changes stays as it is. This is the frame axiom. In LPS nobody writes it, and nobody computes it: the state is simply updated.

Times are written only where a rule relates two moments: the registration comes after the declaration, at some later time. In the program the conclusion of the reactive rule is on one line. Full age and capacity, the prescribed manner, and subsection (4) on wartime are left out, to keep the slide short. Each of them would be one more condition.
